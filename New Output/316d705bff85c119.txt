Facebook founder Mark Zuckerberg is now in congressional crosshairs — with his company’s stock plunging and throngs of users ditching his social media service — amid the growing privacy scandal from the Cambridge Analytica controversy.

And the question of what to do with Zuckerberg’s unregulated internet behemoth could even bring about rare bipartisan action on Capitol Hill.

“You have an issue that has now drawn outrage from the right and the left,” said Maurice Stucke, an antitrust attorney in Washington, D.C. “You have increasing concerns about privacy as well as economic power, so this is a ripe opportunity for change.”

Citing unnamed sources, CNN reported yesterday that Zuckerberg has “come to terms” with the fact that he’ll have to testify within weeks. A Facebook representative said the company has invitations to appear before Congress and is talking to legislators but would not confirm Zuckerberg’s attendance.

Zuckerberg told CNN last week he’d be “happy to” testify. The company is facing unprecedented scrutiny after reports that Cambridge Analytica, a data mining firm, used ill-gotten data from more than 50 million Facebook users to target political ads in a bid to sway elections.

A House Energy and Commerce Committee spokeswoman said yesterday reports of Zuckerberg’s confirmed attendance are “incorrect.” But she said the committee is “continuing to work with Facebook to determine a day and time for Mr. Zuckerberg to testify.”

Federal Trade Commission regulators have begun to scrutinize Facebook’s practices.

Thousands of people who once shared family details, news links and political views on Facebook have pledged on Twitter to scrub it from their lives. Facebook stock plunged yesterday, closing down nearly 5 percent.

“Reasonable people should be concerned that there appears to be very little protection,” said Vivek Krishnamurthy of Harvard Law School’s Cyberlaw Clinic. “Vast amounts of data are being collected and they seem to be moving between companies with very little protection.”

Both Senate and House committees have invited Zuckerberg to testify.

“Mark Zuckerberg needs to testify about the massive data breach,” said U.S. Sen. Edward Markey (D-Mass.). “The privacy of every American depends upon detailed oversight from the Senate to get to the bottom of what Facebook knew, when they knew about it and what they will do to fix it.”

A number of state attorneys general are investigating into Facebook, including Massachusetts Attorney General Maura Healey.

“Companies that control huge amounts of personal data have a legal obligation to guard against theft and misuse of that information,” Healey said in a statement. “We are investigating to find out how and why this data was shared by Facebook and whether the appropriate steps were taken to protect it against misuse and manipulation.”
